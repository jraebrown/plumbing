Pre-Work Approvals & Mandatory Consents

TL;DR: Secure written freeholder consent, notify your home insurer, and get plumber approval before buying or cutting anything.

  • ✅ Freeholder Consent: Obtain formal written permission to alter above-surface pipework.
  • ✅ Insurer Notification: Email your insurer confirming DIY water-side work only; Gas Safe handles the boiler.
  • 🛑 Stop Condition: Do not order materials, drill, or cut until professional route approval is recorded.

First-Floor Flat Water-Escape Risk

Because you live above another flat, any leak poses a catastrophic risk to the property directly below. Proceeding without consent could breach your lease and invalidate your buildings insurance.

The Gas Safe Boundary

You can legally cut/join copper flow and return pipes. You cannot touch the gas supply pipe or disconnect the boiler's gas connection. Only a Gas Safe engineer can do this.

Stage 2 of 10

Route Measurement & Mapping

TL;DR: Physically measure the route on site; never rely solely on floor plans.

  • ✅ Measure Physical Runs: Record each proposed route segment including vertical changes and wall penetrations.
  • ✅ Record Differences: Note flow and return routes separately where their paths differ.
  • ✅ Verify Outer Diameter: Measure the existing boiler stubs with a metal vernier caliper.

The 10% Waste Rule

Calculate total pipe by doubling the one-way distance for both flow and return lines, then add 10% for cutting waste. (e.g. 22.8m one-way = 45.6m + 10% = ~51m).

15mm Outside Diameter (OD)

15mm refers to the outside diameter; ensure domestic-hot-water pipes (usually 22mm) remain untouched.

Stage 3 of 10

Safe Draining & Isolation

TL;DR: Isolate power, cool, drain to 0 bar, and protect flooring from black magnetite sludge.

  • ✅ Power Down: Switch off the fused spur and use a voltage tester to verify zero power.
  • ✅ Cool & Protect: Wait 1 hour for cooling. Lay towels and prepare a wet vacuum for spills.
  • ✅ Connect & Drain: Attach garden hose to drain valve, open radiator bleed valves, and drain slowly.
  • 🛑 Verify Zero Pressure: The boiler pressure gauge must read exactly 0 bar before cutting any pipework.

Magnetite & Chemical Risks

System water contains black iron oxide sludge (magnetite) and chemical inhibitors. This will permanently stain carpets and flooring if spilled. Always ensure the hose is secure and bucket is ready.

Stage 4 of 10

Route and Penetrations

TL;DR: Scan walls before drilling, use sleeves for holes, and secure strictly two pipes with rubber-lined clips.

  • ✅ Scan & Mark: Use a multi-detector to scan BOTH sides of every partition wall before marking.
  • ✅ Drill Penetrations: Confirm hole diameters and sleeve methods with a plumber to account for insulation.
  • ✅ Secure Clips: Fit sleeves and rubber-lined cushioned clips to reduce 'ticking' noises from thermal expansion.

Hidden Electrical Hazards

Cables often run vertically and horizontally from sockets—exactly where skirting board clips are installed. Scan relentlessly.

Approved Support Spacing

Install clips at approximately 600mm horizontal and 1.2m vertical intervals.

Stage 5 of 10

Push-Fit Preparation: The Golden Sequence

TL;DR: Every joint lives or dies by pipe preparation. Cut square, deburr, clean, mark depth, and push.

  • 🔧 1. Cut Square: Use the Rothenberger Pipeslice only. NEVER use a hacksaw.
  • 🔧 2. Deburr: Use a reamer on the inside and outside edge to prevent O-ring damage.
  • ⭐ 3. Clean & Inspect: Wipe the pipe end clean. Ensure it is round and scratch-free.
  • 📝 4. Mark Depth: Measure and mark the 23mm insertion depth with a pen.
  • ✅ 5. Push to Mark: Push firmly until the marker meets the collar, then pull-check.

Protecting the O-Ring

A hacksaw leaves a ragged, angled edge. When you push this edge into a Tectite Sprint fitting, it slices the internal rubber O-ring. It might hold pressure initially, but it will eventually fail. Behind boxing in a first-floor flat, this is a catastrophic disaster.

Stage 6 of 10

Radiator Branchwork

TL;DR: Use parallel equal tees to branch up to radiators R1 and R2 while main trunks continue.

  • ✅ Continuous Mains: The 15mm main trunks must continue past radiators R1 & R2.
  • ✅ Tee Insertion: Cut the mains and insert 15x15x15mm Tectite Equal Tees.
  • ✅ Upward Branches: Run short vertical copper stubs up to the radiator valve inlets.
  • 🛑 Confirm TRV side: Do not infer flow direction from left/right valve position. The engineer must confirm TRV (flow side) orientation on site.

Parallel Connection

Each radiator bridges independently between flow and return mains to ensure proper circulation. Connecting radiators in series will result in sequential heat loss and a cold final radiator.

Stage 7 of 10

Terminal Loop Closure (R3)

TL;DR: The physical loop ends at Bedroom 2. Use elbows (not tees), then trace the return back to the boiler.

  • ✅ No Tees at R3: The trunks hit 90° terminal elbows and turn directly up into the R3 radiator valves.
  • ✅ The Bridge: Do NOT join the red flow and blue return pipes directly together. The radiator itself acts as the final bridge.
  • ✅ Continuous Return: Follow the blue return pipe continuously back to the boiler to ensure it is unbroken.

Visual Verification

Visual inspection cannot establish that a system is leak-free before it is filled. This stage is a visible-installation check only to ensure the return path is continuous and no accidental dead-ends exist.

Stage 8 of 10

Professional Reconnection

TL;DR: Present your visible pipework, step back, and let the Gas Safe professional handle the boiler internals.

  • 🛑 STOP - Engineer Required: The engineer must positively identify flow/return on-site using the Glow-worm manual.
  • ✅ Boundary: Do NOT cut or reconnect boiler pipes based on diagrams or handle colours yourself.
  • ✅ Record: Capture the engineer’s name, Gas Safe registration number, and the chosen connection method.

You own the water. The professional owns the gas.

Connecting gas appliances without Gas Safe registration is illegal and will void your home insurance. The homeowner prepares the visible copper stubs; the engineer physically reconnects them to the boiler block.

Stage 9 of 10

Pressure Testing & Dosing

TL;DR: Fill system, check every single joint relentlessly with blue roll, and apply inhibitor.

  • ✅ System Fill: Fill slowly via the braided loop to the engineer-specified pressure (usually 1.0-1.5 bar).
  • ✅ Bleed: Systematically bleed radiators (R1, R2, R3).
  • ⭐ The Blue Roll Test: Wipe dry blue tissue around EVERY single push-fit and compression nut.
  • ✅ Dosing: Add chemical inhibitor as specified by the engineer.
  • 🛑 Dampness = Failure: Any dampness on the blue roll requires immediate resolution. Do not conceal a weeping joint.

Never Box In Too Early

Keep pipework fully exposed until the agreed monitoring period (hot and cold cycles) has passed without unresolved pressure drops or leaks.

Stage 10 of 10

Aesthetic Boxing In

TL;DR: Hide the pipes, but never bury the joints. Ensure expansion gaps and access hatches.

  • ✅ Frame: Fix 25x38mm timber battens securely above and below the pipe run.
  • ✅ Fascia: Use 160mm wide MDF strips to allow room for future water pipes.
  • ✅ Thermal Gap: Leave 5-10mm expansion gaps at the ends of the run (copper expands when heated).
  • 🛑 The Golden Rule: Magnetic access hatches (min 150x150mm) are strictly required at every single fitting/valve location. Never permanently bury a joint.

Thermal Movement

Copper pipes expand when heated. A 5m run of copper pipe will expand by approximately 5mm when heated from cold to operating temperature. If you box them in tightly with expanding foam or tight wood, they will buckle or snap the push-fit O-rings.
