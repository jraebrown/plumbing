You live above another flat. A water leak is a five-figure insurance claim in your neighbour's ceiling. Do NOT order pipe, cut, or drill until freeholder consent, insurer notification, and plumber approval are all confirmed in writing.

1. Checking Your Lease

Most flat leases require freeholder or managing agent consent before altering services. Search your lease for "alterations", "services", or "consent". Email your managing agent: "Do I need consent to install above-surface pipework to replace a failed embedded heating circuit?" Get the response in writing.

⚠️ If you proceed without consent: You may breach your lease, invalidate your buildings insurance, be personally liable for damage to the flat below, and face a dilapidations claim when you sell.

2. Notifying Your Insurer

What to say: "I am planning to install above-surface 15mm copper pipework to replace a failed embedded heating circuit in my first-floor flat. The work involves connecting to existing copper stubs above the concrete floor and running new pipe along walls to three radiators. I will not be touching any gas connections. Please confirm this work is covered under my policy."

3. The Gas Safe Boundary

You CAN doCut and join copper flow/return pipes. Fit push-fit fittings. Replace radiator valves. Refill and bleed the system.
You CANNOT doTouch the gas supply pipe. Adjust the gas valve or burner. Disconnect or reconnect the boiler's gas connection.
Book nowContact a Gas Safe engineer now and book them for Stage 8. They need to be available when your pipework is complete.

4. Identifying Flow and Return at the Boiler

Before draining the system, run the heating briefly and feel the pipes near the boiler. The flow pipe (hot water going out) will be noticeably hotter. Label both pipes with tape: "FLOW" and "RETURN". This label stays on until the system is fully commissioned.

Safe Draining & Isolation

Stage 3 of 10 · Isolate power · Cool · Drain · Verify 0 bar
⏱ 1.5 hrs Medium
Isolate powerDrain to bucketBleed valves

Isolate electrical power, allow the system to cool completely, and drain the heating circuit safely before touching any pipework.

⚠️ Critical Safety

Never drain a hot system. Scalding water under pressure is a serious burn risk. Always verify the boiler pressure gauge reads zero before cutting any pipe. Water may contain black magnetite sludge — protect carpets and the flat below.

1

Switch off the boiler at the programmer/timer and at the fused spur. Confirm dead with a voltage tester.

2

Allow the system to cool for at least 1 hour after the last heating cycle.

3

Lay protective towels and prepare a wet/dry vacuum for spills.

4

Attach a garden hose to the drain-off valve. Route to a drain or bucket outside.

5

Open radiator bleed valves slightly (one turn anti-clockwise) to speed drainage.

6

Open the drain-off valve slowly. Wait until flow stops completely (20–40 minutes).

✅ Complete When
  • Boiler isolated at fused spur — indicator light confirmed off
  • System fully cooled — at least 1 hour after last heating cycle
  • All water drained — zero flow from drain valve
  • Boiler pressure gauge reads 0 bar
  • Bleed valves closed again after draining

Detailed Draining Steps

  1. Switch off the boiler at the programmer/timer.
  2. Switch off the boiler at the fused spur. Confirm dead with a voltage tester.
  3. Allow the system to cool for at least 1 hour. Do not rush this step.
  4. Locate the drain-off valve — a small brass valve, usually at the lowest point of the system, often near the boiler. It has a square or slotted head.
  5. Attach a garden hose to the drain-off valve outlet (standard hose thread). Run the hose to a drain, outside, or into buckets.
  6. Open all radiator bleed valves slightly (one turn anti-clockwise with a bleed key). This lets air in and speeds drainage significantly.
  7. Open the drain-off valve slowly with a flat-head screwdriver or square drain key. Water will flow. It may be discoloured — this is normal.
  8. Wait until the flow stops completely. This can take 20–40 minutes for a full system.
  9. Close the drain-off valve once empty. Keep towels and wet vacuum ready for residual water when pipes are cut.
💡 Tip: Some water will remain trapped in the radiators and low points of the pipework. Have old towels ready when you cut the pipes — a small amount of water will spill. A wet/dry vacuum is useful for clearing water from around the cut area.

Cable Detection

Use a multi-detector (cable, pipe, and stud finder) such as the Bosch Truvo. Scan both sides of every partition wall before drilling. Scan in multiple directions — cables can run horizontally as well as vertically. Isolate electrical supplies where appropriate before drilling near socket positions.

Wall Penetrations

Drill through partition walls at skirting board level to keep pipes low and make boxing-in simpler. The hole must accommodate the pipe plus any sleeve, grommet, insulation, and expansion clearance. Confirm the exact hole size with your plumber before drilling.

🔥 Fire-Stopping: If the partition wall is a fire-resisting wall, the penetration must be fire-stopped. Confirm with your plumber whether intumescent sealant or a fire-stopping sleeve is required.

05

Push-Fit Preparation — The Golden Sequence

Stage 5 of 10 · Cut · Deburr · Clean · Mark · Push
⏱ 4 hrs Medium–High
Cut squareDeburrClean & inspect ⭐Mark depthPush to mark

Every Tectite Sprint connection lives or dies by pipe preparation. A clean, square, deburred end pushed to the full insertion depth is the entire secret to a leak-free joint.

⚠️ Safety-Critical — Read Before Every Cut

NEVER use a hacksaw. It leaves a ragged edge that will damage the O-ring seal. In a first-floor flat, a failed joint behind boxing is a five-figure problem.

1
CUT SQUAREUse the Rothenberger Pipeslice only. Rotate around the pipe, tightening slightly each turn. Never use a hacksaw.
2
DEBURR INSIDE + OUTSIDEUse a proper internal/external reamer. The pipe slice leaves a burr on both the inside and outside of the cut end.
3
CLEAN AND INSPECT ⭐ Most commonly skipped — do not skip itWipe the pipe end clean. Remove all swarf, debris, and contamination. Inspect the tube end — it must be round, undamaged, and free of scratches.
4
MARK INSERTION DEPTHMeasure the insertion depth from the fitting packaging (verify — do not assume). Mark with a permanent marker.
5
PUSH TO MARK AND VERIFYPush the pipe firmly into the fitting until the marker meets the fitting collar. Try to pull it back — it should not move.
✅ Complete When (per joint)
  • Cut is square — no angle, no hacksaw marks
  • Inside and outside edges fully deburred
  • Pipe end clean, dry, and free of debris
  • Exact insertion depth verified from fitting packaging
  • Pipe seated to full depth — depth mark flush with fitting body
  • Joint inspected — pipe does not pull back out under moderate force

How to Check a Tectite Fitting is Properly Seated

After pushing the pipe in, try to pull it back out with moderate force. It should not move. If it pulls out easily, the grab ring has not engaged — push it in again more firmly. You can also draw a line on the pipe at the fitting edge before inserting it, then check the line is flush with the fitting body after insertion.

Demounting a Tectite Sprint Fitting

Tectite Sprint fittings are NOT demountable. If you need to redo a connection, you must cut the pipe and use a new fitting. This is why buying spare fittings is essential. If you want the option to release fittings, use Tectite Classic (demountable) instead.

Radiators R1 and R2 — Mid-Loop Tee Connections

At Radiators R1 (Reception Room) and R2 (Bedroom 1), cut the flow main and insert a Tectite Sprint Equal Tee, running a short vertical copper stub up to the radiator valve. Do the same on the return main. Both main trunks continue past each radiator.

  1. Mark the tee position on both flow and return pipes. The tee branch should align with the radiator valve positions.
  2. Cut the flow pipe at the marked position. Push the Tectite Tee onto both cut ends.
  3. Cut a short piece of copper to run from the tee branch up to the radiator valve. Deburr, polish, push into the tee branch.
  4. Connect to the radiator valve using the compression nut. Tighten with two spanners (one holds the valve body, one tightens the nut).
  5. Repeat for the return pipe.

Route Continuity Check

Trace each main through its tees to confirm it does not terminate at a radiator. Follow each radiator valve back to its tee and confirm that one branch reaches the flow route and the other the return route.

Two adjustable spanners (for radiator valve compression nuts)

R3 Terminal Connection

Radiator R3 in Bedroom 2 is the furthest point in the circuit and the end of the loop. Unlike R1 and R2 which use tees (with the main trunks continuing past), R3 uses 90° elbows to turn each pipe up into the respective valve inlet.

  1. Run both pipes along the wall to Radiator R3.
  2. At the radiator position, cut the flow pipe and push on a 90° Elbow to turn it upward toward the flow valve.
  3. Cut a short vertical piece of copper to run from the elbow up to the valve. Connect with the compression nut.
  4. Do the same for the return pipe — Elbow turning upward into the lockshield valve.
  5. Tighten compression nuts with two spanners.

Checking the Return Route

Follow the return pipe from R3 back towards the boiler area, checking for hidden tees or changes of direction. The return should remain a continuous path that eventually reaches the confirmed boiler return connection. Record the route in your notes.

💡 Record everything: Use the notes section to record sketches, photographs, and questions for the plumber. Note where isolation valves, radiator tails, tees, and other components are located.

The Blue Roll Wipe Test

Take a piece of dry blue roll or tissue paper and wipe it around every single fitting — every Tectite push-fit, every compression nut on the radiator valves, every coupler. The tissue must come away completely dry. Any dampness, however slight, indicates a leak.

⚠️ If you find a leak: Do not ignore it. Do not box in over it. Do not fire the boiler. Isolate or drain as necessary. Have the fitting inspected and corrected in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

Venting Radiators

Vent each radiator as required. Water clarity alone is not a reliable indication that the system is clean. Start with the radiator closest to the boiler and work outward to R3 (Bedroom 2).

Inhibitor Dosing

Clean, refill, and dose with a compatible inhibitor as specified by the heating engineer. Record the product, dose, and inhibitor-test result. BS 7593 is relevant guidance; it does not require a specific brand. The BoilerMag filter should be cleaned before refilling.

Observation Period

Agree with the engineer how long the system should be observed after testing, and at what times or conditions it should be checked. Boxing should wait until this agreed period has passed without unresolved concerns.

Boxing In

Stage 10 of 10 · Last step · 160mm wide · Access hatches · Expansion gaps
⏱ 4–5 hrs Medium
Install battens & panelAccess hatchFinished result

Finish the appearance of proven pipe runs without hiding joints or components that need future access. Do NOT box in until the agreed monitoring period is complete and any defects are resolved.

⚠️ Low Priority — Last Step

Build the boxing 160mm wide (not 80mm) to leave room for future water pipes alongside the heating pipes. Never permanently bury inaccessible joints or valves.

1

Wait for completed monitoring and engineer approval before starting any boxing.

2

Photograph the pipe route and mark every valve and joint location before enclosing.

3

Fix 25×38mm timber battens to the wall above and below the pipe run.

4

5

6

Fill, sand, prime, and paint to match the existing skirting.

✅ Complete When
  • Testing and the agreed observation period are complete
  • Boxing design measured against the actual pipe route
  • Required joints, valves, controls, and service points remain accessible
  • Boxing does not damage, stress, or restrict the pipework
  • Finished design can be removed or opened where future access is required

Materials

25×38mm sawn timber battens (B&Q or timber merchant)
6mm MDF smooth board — cut to 160mm wide strips on site
Grab adhesive or screws + wall plugs
Decorator's caulk (white)
Paint to match wall or skirting
Magnetic access panel catches (one per fitting location)

Construction Steps

  1. Fix 25×38mm timber battens to the wall above and below the pipe run using screws and wall plugs.
  2. Cut 6mm MDF strips to 160mm wide (not 80mm — leave room for future water pipes). Test-fit before fixing.
  3. At every union, tee, elbow, coupler, or valve, cut a removable access hatch (minimum 150×150mm). Fit a magnetic catch.
  4. Screw MDF strips to battens. Countersink screws flush for a clean finish.
  5. Fill, sand, prime, and paint to match the existing skirting.

Service Zones

Think in terms of "service zones": note where isolation valves, radiator tails, tees, filters, or other components are, and make sure boxing can be removed or opened at those points without demolition. Record the locations and any fixings that need to be undone.

💡 Thermal Expansion: Copper pipes expand when heated. A 5m run of copper pipe will expand by approximately 5mm when heated from cold (10°C) to operating temperature (70°C). Leave a 5–10mm gap at each end of the boxing run. Do NOT use expanding foam inside the boxing — it makes future access impossible.
